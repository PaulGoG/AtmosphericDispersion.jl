Known limitations

An independent review of the physics against the literature, in November 2026, and the published text of CNCAN NSR-23 (Comisia Națională pentru Controlul Activităților Nucleare, 2004) led to the corrections of 1.1.0: the stable limits of the plume rise in the stable classes only, washout for the travel time, a wet deposition on the share of the release in the precipitation, the governing building and the limit on the wake of a ground-level release, the 1000 m limit of the Eimutis–Konicek scheme, the cap of the combined rise and the minimum wind speed. The changelog lists them with their effect on the reference case. The points below remain.

Bracketing values and the activity budget

Depletion of the airborne plume takes the low deposition velocity and the low washout coefficient, and deposition the high ones, as NSR-23 Tables 6 and 7 direct. Each choice is conservative for its own pathway. Dry deposition is the high velocity times an air concentration depleted with the low velocity and the low washout coefficient, so the ground receives up to the ratio of the two velocities times what dry deposition takes from the plume, 2 for HTO. Wet deposition is the largest deposit the precipitation can lay at each distance, undepleted on the way, so its integral over the ground, f A Λ_H X/u out to X, exceeds the activity released into the precipitation beyond X = u/Λ_H, 10 km for HTO in rain at 5 mm/h and 6 m/s. The budgets are separate: material that wet deposition removes is still in the air that feeds dry deposition, so with precipitation the two deposits together can exceed what the plume loses.

Washout of a short precipitation

Washout of the airborne plume takes the material released into the precipitation to stay in it for its whole travel time, the limit of a precipitation much longer than that. For a shorter precipitation fixed over the site the result depends on when it falls, which a configuration does not state: for Λ = 10⁻³ s⁻¹, 900 s of precipitation in a 3600 s release and 20 km at 6 m/s, the surviving fraction lies between 0.47 and 0.92 by timing, against 0.76 from the form. Where Λ x/u is large the precipitation must outlast the travel time by far, min(t_w, T_R) ≫ (exp(Λ x/u) − 1)/Λ, since what falls before or after the release scavenges nothing of it. load_configuration warns when, of the timings that hold the share f of the release, the one that leaves the most airborne (beginning with the release, or ending with it) would leave more than 10 % more than the form at the edge of the receptor grid. Setting no washout bounds the air concentration for any timing. Wet deposition is not affected: it is the bound for any timing.

Dry deposition of tritiated water

A deposition velocity above zero depletes the plume and lowers the air concentration downwind. Deposited HTO is re-emitted, and Lee et al. (2012) conclude that an effective deposition velocity of zero is the conservative choice for safety analyses, after the 0.5 cm/s of the MACCS2 guidance had been questioned as not bounding. IAEA SRS-19, EUR 15760 and HPA-RPD-058 also take zero. The package follows NSR-23, whose notes to Table 6 give 0.4–0.8 cm/s; setting both velocities to zero gives the conservative air concentration.

Buildings

These are the rules of NSR-23 §6.2, which the package follows.

  • A release below the top of the governing building is entrained into the cavity and released at ground level, and the plume rise is then added, as NSR-23 prescribes. For a 60 m building 25 m from the reference stack the effective height in class D is 120 m. The buoyancy flux is not lost when the plume is mixed into the cavity, but the rise of a plume that leaves a cavity is smaller than that of one leaving a stack; IAEA SRS-19 takes the release height as zero throughout the wake zone, which it calls pessimistic.
  • Between one and two and a half building heights, the entrainment test depends on whether the mean wind at the stack, averaged over the six classes, exceeds 5 m/s, so the release height changes discontinuously at that wind speed.
  • The √3 limit on the wake broadening applies to a plume at effective height zero, a release at ground level with no plume rise, the case for which NRC XOQDOQ and NSR-23 §5 state it. A plume with any rise, including one entrained into the cavity and released at ground level, is broadened without limit, as NSR-23 Eq. (9) prescribes: for a plume well above the ground the limit would lower the ground-level concentration, in class D by a factor of 9 in the sector average at 200 m for a plume at 50 m beside a 60 m building. Below a height of two to four σ_z it would raise it, so the concentration drops between no plume rise and the smallest. The limit binds while σ < √(C A/2π): for the 60 m building of 3600 m² beside the reference stack, σ_z out to 0.54 km in class D and 2.6 km in class F, and, for a release of ten minutes, σ_y out to 0.30 and 0.62 km (the meander of a longer release widens σ_y past it). At 200 m a stack with the smallest efflux gives a lower sector-averaged concentration than one with none by a factor of 2.0 in class D and 5.1 in class F, and, for a ten-minute release, a lower centreline value by 2.7 and 13.
  • The vicinity of a building is judged from the point given for it, which should be the point of the building nearest the stack; the package carries no footprint.

Plume rise

  • Where the two final rises agree within 10 % the combined law applies, otherwise the law of the larger mechanism alone. The far-field rise is continuous at the edge of that band; the near-field rise is not.
  • The transitional momentum law is NSR-23 Eq. (21), written for an efflux of ambient density, while the combined law takes the density ratio through the momentum flux; for the reference density ratio of 0.5 the two differ by a factor of 1.26. The reference case is dominated by buoyancy.
  • exit_temperature is not used: the buoyancy flux is formed from the two densities, as in NSR-23 Eq. (17.b), and the two are not checked against each other. The reference pair, 0.6 kg/m³ at 324 K, is lighter than steam at that temperature.
  • The stable classes E and F share the one temperature gradient of the Atmosphere. EPA ISC3 takes 0.020 K/m of potential temperature for E and 0.035 K/m for F where no measurement is available; the reference gradient, 0.02 K/m of temperature, is 0.030 K/m of potential temperature.

Meander

The broadening of σ_y for a release longer than ten minutes, (t_R/600)^0.2, is defined in NSR-23 Eq. (8) between ten minutes and one hour, for which the normative recommends the sector-averaged form beyond. The package applies it at every duration. The Handbook on Atmospheric Diffusion gives an exponent of 0.25 to 0.3 between 1 h and 100 h.
